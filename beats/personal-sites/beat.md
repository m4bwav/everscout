# Personal and professional websites

Follows how people, especially developers, designers and other tech workers, think about and build their own websites and blogs: portfolios, personal homepages, blogs, digital gardens, "now" pages, and the small-web or IndieWeb revival. It covers the ideas (why own a site at all, what it is for, what it should say) and the craft (layout, typography, motion, stacks, hosting). Built to refresh one person's professional site with current, well-received ideas, and to seed a skill that designs personal sites.

## Scope

- In: personal portfolios and homepages of developers, designers, writers and other tech people; personal blogs and newsletters on one's own domain; digital gardens and "now", "uses", colophon and other slash pages; IndieWeb practice (own your domain, POSSE, webmentions, RSS, webrings, blogrolls); visual and interaction trends as applied to personal sites (typography, bento grids, dark mode, motion, texture, brutalism, retro "old web"); static site generators and hosting for personal sites; how hiring managers and clients read portfolios; AI-built sites and the reaction to them.
- Out: business and e-commerce sites, agency marketing, SEO and growth for commercial blogs, template and theme sales, general web-dev questions unrelated to a personal site, social-media-only personal branding.

## What we want to learn

1. What does a well-received personal or professional site look like now (structure, pages, content), and how does it differ for a developer, a designer and a writer?
2. Which visual and interaction ideas are current on personal sites (typography, layout, motion, texture, playfulness), and which are wearing out?
3. Why do people keep or drop a personal site in 2026: hiring, clients, writing, owning their work, leaving social platforms?
4. Which stacks and hosts do people choose for personal sites, and why (static generators, frameworks, no-code builders, AI builders)?
5. What do hiring managers, recruiters and clients actually look at on a portfolio?
6. How is the IndieWeb or small-web revival changing personal sites (RSS, webrings, blogrolls, webmentions, slash pages)?

## Keep and skip

- Keep: people showing their own site with the reasoning behind it; redesign write-ups and colophons; critiques and portfolio-review threads with substantive answers; hiring-manager comments on portfolios; essays on why to have a personal site; curated galleries of personal sites; new tools aimed at personal sites.
- Skip: "rate my portfolio" posts with no replies, template ads, generic trend listicles from site builders unless independent sources agree, SEO spam.

## Engagement notes

The makers on this beat are people who show their own site. Ask about the decisions (why this layout, what they cut, what the site has done for them), never pitch a service. r/web_design and r/webdev limit self-promotion (r/webdev keeps it to Showoff Saturday); read the rules before posting. Hacker News bans generated comments. IndieWeb people prefer replies on their own sites (webmentions) or the fediverse.
