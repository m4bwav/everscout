# Down-tempo, trip-hop and chill electronic music

Follows the people who make slow electronic music (down-tempo, trip-hop, chillout, ambient-leaning, lo-fi and its neighbours): new releases, how producers build their tracks, the labels and scenes, and how the music finds listeners. Producers posting their own work are the core; reviews and label news are context.

## Scope

- In: producers sharing their own tracks with gear, sampling or mixing detail; album and EP releases by artists in the scene and how listeners react; production technique discussions (sampling, texture, drums, space, mixing slow music); labels and their releases; the trip-hop revival; the business (Bandcamp, streaming, labels changing hands); platform rules on AI music.
- Out: fast dance genres, generic playlist promotion, AI-generated music channels, gear deals, "rate my playlist".

## What we want to learn

1. What are producers in the scene making tracks with now (DAWs, samplers, synths, texture plugins), and how do they get the sound?
2. Which artists, labels and releases are people talking about, and which are rising?
3. How do independent producers find listeners in 2026 (Bandcamp, streaming, YouTube channels, labels, live)?
4. How is the trip-hop revival sounding and who is behind it?
5. How are communities and platforms handling AI-generated music, and how does that change what producers disclose?

## Keep and skip

- Keep: a producer's own track or album with anything said about how it was made; release news from artists and labels in scope; production discussions with specifics; label and platform business news; threads about AI music policy.
- Skip: playlist promotion, "follow for follow", AI music channels, reposts of old classics with no discussion, genre-name arguments with no music.

## Engagement notes

Music communities are broadly hostile to AI music. Bandcamp banned music made wholly or substantially by AI (2026-01-13); r/LofiHipHop bans it; r/WeAreTheMusicMakers bans AI posts and allows self-promotion only in its weekly thread; r/ambientmusic has frequent "is this AI?" threads. Never ask a musician whether a track used AI: it reads as an accusation. Ask about gear, sampling, texture, mixing, composition and release; producers love those questions. Keep drafts short and warm, and never promote anything. Forums (Elektronauts, KVR) value long, human replies; write those by hand.
