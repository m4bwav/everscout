# Global economics and macro

Follows how economists, analysts and data people read the world economy: central banks, inflation, labour markets, trade and tariffs, energy, debt and development. The unit of interest is an argument backed by data, a new paper or dataset, or a policy decision and how practitioners interpret it. Market chatter and partisan takes are noise unless practitioners take them up.

## Scope

- In: central bank decisions and speeches (Fed, ECB, BoE, BoJ, PBoC); data releases and how analysts read them (CPI, payrolls, GDP, PMIs); new papers and working papers; charts with their sources; trade policy and its measured effects; IMF, World Bank, OECD and BIS outlooks; development and emerging-market debt; long-form analysis from economists' newsletters.
- Out: stock picks and trading; crypto; partisan outrage without data; personal finance; doom threads.

## What we want to learn

1. What do central bankers and the analysts who watch them signal before a policy turn, and how well did it read in hindsight?
2. How are tariffs and the 2026 energy shock showing up in prices, trade flows and employment, measured by whom?
3. Which indicators and datasets do practitioners trust now, and which revisions or methods are they arguing about?
4. What do economists disagree about (soft landing, labour hoarding, AI and productivity, debt sustainability), and which evidence moves them?
5. Which new papers, datasets and tools (chart workflows, nowcasts) are practitioners adopting?

## Keep and skip

- Keep: a data release with practitioners' reading of it; a chart or paper by its author with source and method; policy decisions with the vote and the statement; disagreements between named economists with evidence; revisions and methodology changes.
- Skip: headlines without data, market predictions, political fights, "the economy is collapsing" posts, questions with no substantive answers.

## Engagement notes

r/Economics requires comments to engage with the article; r/AskEconomics accepts only in-depth answers from people who know; r/badeconomics wants written explanations. So engagement here is mostly questions to authors of charts, papers and data posts: r/dataisbeautiful (OC posts must state source and tool, and chart makers answer), r/econometrics (method questions welcome), r/econmonitor, r/EconomicHistory, and economists on Bluesky and econtwitter.net. Good questions ask about the data series, the deflator, seasonal adjustment, the sample window and what would change the author's mind. Never argue a political point; never answer from memory (conduct rule 14).
