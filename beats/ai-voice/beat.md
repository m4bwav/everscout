# AI voice: speech, design, cloning and singing

Follows people who make voices with models and those who judge the results: open and hosted text to speech, voice design, cloning and conversion, singing voice synthesis, dubbing, cleanup, voice acting craft, and the law around consent. It feeds voicewright. What people finish and what listeners accept matters more than launch news.

Split with `ai-video`: voice models, voice tools and voice craft live here. Audio native to a video model (Veo, Kling or Seedance dialogue, lip sync as part of a video pipeline) stays in `ai-video`. Instrumental music stays with `downtempo` and any future music beat; a singing voice model is here.

## Scope

- In: model and tool releases with people's reported results and side-by-side samples; how to reach a voice that is not a stock preset; consistency across lines and sessions; artifacts and what cleans them; finished narration, game and film work with the method named; voice acting and direction craft; listener and player reaction; store rules, union terms, statutes and cases on voice rights.
- Out: real-time voice changers for pranks; celebrity imitation showcases; vendor listicles; generic AI news; call-centre and IVR products unless a technique carries over.

## What we want to learn

1. Which engines do makers finish real work with, for each job (design, short-clip clone, long narration, acted emotion, dialogue, non-English, real time, singing)?
2. Why do voices collapse into a few sounds, and which methods truly widen the range?
3. How do people keep one voice the same across takes, sessions and machines?
4. What causes noise, metallic sound and level jumps, and which cleanup steps help or hurt?
5. What reads as stereotypical or as AI to listeners, and what moves a voice away from that?
6. How are audiences, stores, unions and courts treating AI voice, and which rules changed?

## Keep and skip

- Keep: a comparison with samples; an engine release thread where people report results; a long troubleshooting thread that ends in a fix; a finished piece with the method named; a performer's or listener's specific objection; a licence, watermark or policy change; a ruling or a statute moving.
- Skip: "what is the best TTS" with no answers worth reading, vendor showreels, resold wrappers, reposted clips, imitations of named people shown for fun.

## Engagement notes
