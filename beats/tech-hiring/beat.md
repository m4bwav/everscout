# Developer and tech hiring

Follows how software developers and other tech workers get hired now, from both sides of the table: job seekers reporting their searches, hiring managers and recruiters describing how they screen, and the data that says where demand is going. The lived process matters as much as the headline numbers: a count of layoffs says less than a hiring manager explaining why they stopped hiring juniors.

## Scope

- In: the software and tech labour market (postings, openings, layoffs, rehiring, seniority mix, AI-titled roles, compensation); interview formats and how they are changing (take-homes, live coding, LeetCode, system design, on-site returns, AI-assisted and AI-proctored interviews, cheating tools and the response to them); screening (ATS, AI résumé filters, referrals, ghost jobs, application volume per opening); search reports from candidates with numbers; hiring-manager and recruiter practice; remote, hybrid and return-to-office as a hiring factor; visas and offshoring where they change hiring (H-1B, India GCCs); entry-level and new-grad prospects.
- Out: individual job ads and "who is hiring" listings (read only as a count), résumé-review requests, generic motivation posts, salary brag threads with no context, bootcamp marketing, non-tech hiring unless it moves the tech market (then the macro data only).

## What we want to learn

1. Where is demand for developers actually rising or falling (by seniority, specialty, region, remote), and does postings data agree with what candidates report?
2. How are interviews changing in response to AI assistants (on-site returns, new formats, allowed-AI rounds, detection), and what do candidates and interviewers think of each?
3. What does a successful search look like now: applications sent, response rate, channels that work (referrals, recruiters, boards, networks), time to offer?
4. How do hiring managers and recruiters screen when every opening gets hundreds of applications, and how much is automated?
5. What is happening to entry-level and new-grad hiring, and what routes still work for newcomers?
6. How are layoffs, rehiring ("boomerang" hires), offshoring and visa rules shifting who gets hired and where?

## Keep and skip

- Keep: search reports with numbers (a Sankey chart, a spreadsheet, a count of applications and offers); hiring-manager or recruiter posts describing their process or volume; interview-format changes at named companies; data releases (Indeed Hiring Lab, JOLTS, TrueUp, layoffs.fyi) and the discussion around them; long threads comparing experiences across companies; policy changes (visa fees, pay-transparency laws, AI-in-hiring laws).
- Skip: "rate my résumé", "should I learn X", single-line venting, job ads, referral trading, course and coaching marketing, reposted listicles.

## Engagement notes

The "makers" on this beat are people who publish their own search data or their own hiring process. r/cscareerquestions and r/csMajors are open to questions about a poster's search; r/ExperiencedDevs lets only developers with three or more years post or comment outside the weekly Ask thread, so engage there only if the user qualifies. r/recruitinghell is venting and sentiment: read only. r/recruiting and r/EngineeringManagers are practitioner communities: ask about process, never pitch. Hacker News bans generated comments. Ask about the numbers and the method, never for the poster's employer or identity. Many "I analyzed 10,000 jobs" posts are marketing for a job tool; check before asking.
